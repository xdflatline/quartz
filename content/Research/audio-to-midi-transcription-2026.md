# Audio-to-MIDI transcription: Dettato and the architecture thesis

**Source:** [[Raw/dettato-audio-to-midi-2026-11-16]] — kobimusic, 2026-11-16
**Status:** Vendor-run benchmarks and a single-author architecture argument. Not independently replicated.

## The problem

Automatic music transcription (AMT) converts an audio recording into note events — pitch, onset, duration, velocity, and ideally instrument. It is hard for reasons that compound:

- **Polyphony.** Multiple simultaneous notes share an overlapping harmonic series; separating them is an underdetermined problem.
- **Timbre.** The same pitch looks different on piano, strings and voice; a model must generalize across instruments it may never have heard.
- **Instrument attribution.** Even when notes are found, deciding *which* instrument played them is a separate classification problem.
- **Expressive timing.** Rubato, swing and tempo drift mean the "correct" onset is ambiguous.
- **Offsets.** Note endings are masked by decay and reverb and are consistently the weakest measure.
- **Notation.** Turning note events into a readable score is a further, largely separate problem.

## What Dettato claims

A 20M-parameter open-source model, trained on no copyrighted recordings, reporting:

- **Composite 60.8** versus 56.1 for Mirelo A2M v1.1 Pro (closed), 47.4 for MuScriptor (1.3B, open) and 45.7 for YourMT3+ (46M).
- **Roughly 65x fewer parameters** than the strongest open baseline.
- **Commercial use of outputs permitted**, code open source.

It is the first entry in the garden for this task, so the page also serves as the AMT reference point.

## The architecture thesis

The author's account is the most transferable part of the release:

> "I treated AMT as a data problem - but in reality, the bottleneck is architecture."

After roughly ten months, the first approach was scrapped in April 2026. The replacement reframes transcription as a **local** problem: model each pitch as a lane and decide onset, sustain and offset within that lane, rather than generating a whole transcription globally. See [[Concepts/pitch-lane-transcription-model]].

The same move is claimed to work for **optical music recognition**: where a global model overfits, a detector model wins. The general lesson is that when structured prediction plateaus, the formulation may be the constraint — and a local decision surface can be both smaller and more accurate than a global generative one.

This resonates with a pattern elsewhere in the garden: [[Concepts/decision-models-as-classifiers]] makes the same trade for language-model judgments, and [[Concepts/purpose-built-memory-model-stack]] for memory operations.

## Training without copyrighted recordings

The corpus is assembled from three sources:

1. **Synthetic instrument recordings** — rendered from known note events, giving exact labels.
2. **CC solo instruments mixed through URMP** — real performances, openly licensed, combined into ensembles.
3. **~20 morphed soundfonts** — additional timbres generated by the author's "soundfont babies" technique.

No copyrighted recordings were used. The author's stronger claim is that the constraint **improved** the model, because it removed the option of brute-forcing the problem with a scraped corpus and pushed the work into architecture. See [[Concepts/copyright-clean-training-data]].

The synthetic-to-real gap is the open question: the reported real-recording scores are the counter-evidence, but they come from the same vendor and skew classical/small-ensemble.

## How to read the numbers

- **Frames are the easiest measure, offsets the hardest.** Dettato scores 79.1 frames and 40.9 offsets; the composite 60.8 is a mean of unlike measures, not an accuracy percentage.
- **The composite is set-weighted** over PHENICX, RWC Classical and RWC Royalty-Free — a classical-heavy mix. On RWC Popular the score is 54.0 and on RWC Genre 48.7.
- **Dettato does not lead everywhere.** On RWC Classical it scores 64.4 against Mirelo's 65.8.
- **Synthetic benchmarks flatter synthetic training.** Slakh2100 (68.4) is rendered from MIDI and should not be used as evidence of real-world generalization.
- **Instrument splitting is a stated weakness**, and no headline metric captures it.

Full metric discussion: [[Concepts/transcription-evaluation-metrics]].

## Performance MIDI, not sheet music

The output is beat-aligned performance MIDI. It is suitable for DAW work, analysis, re-performance and MIDI cleanup. It is not a playable score. Notation requires quantization, enharmonic spelling, voice separation, beaming, transposition and layout; the author recommends Songscription or Klangio for that deliverable. See [[Concepts/performance-midi-vs-notation]].

## The competitive map

| System | Status | Parameters | Composite |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dettato | open source | 20M | 60.8 |
| Mirelo A2M v1.1 Pro | closed | not published | 56.1 |
| MuScriptor | open | 1.3B | 47.4 |
| YourMT3+ | open | 46M | 45.7 |

The 20M-versus-1.3B gap is the headline, but note that Mirelo's closed model is within 4.7 points on the composite and ahead on one real dataset. The claim that holds cleanly is **efficiency parity**, not unconditional superiority.

## What generalizes

1. **Question the formulation before buying more data.** Ten months of data work were discarded; the architecture change produced the result.
2. **Local decisions compose into global structure.** Pitch lanes for AMT, detectors for OMR, typed judgments for agent memory.
3. **Synthetic data is viable when labels are exact and the evaluation is per-domain.** The risk is concentrated where the renderer is weakest.
4. **Licensing constraints can select better inductive biases**, because they remove the brute-force option.
5. **State the deliverable boundary.** "Audio to MIDI" and "audio to sheet music" are different products, and saying so protects the benchmark from over-reading.

## Boundaries of this source

- **Single-author vendor post.** Benchmarks are self-reported; no independent replication is cited.
- **Benchmark sets are narrow.** Mostly Western classical, small ensemble and synthetic; pop, jazz and non-Western material are thin (one RWC row each).
- **The comparison scores for other systems are as published by those systems** where Mirelo is concerned, and the author notes Mirelo's numbers are "on their published benchmarks".
- **No ablation** isolates the pitch-lane architecture from the training corpus or the model size.
- **Instrument splitting** is acknowledged as weak but unquantified in the post.
- **The architecture claim is a single data point.** One model succeeding with a local formulation does not establish that global models cannot work with enough data.

## Open questions

- Would the pitch-lane formulation scale with more parameters, or is 20M near its ceiling?
- How much of the result depends on the synthetic corpus matching the test domains?
- Can instrument attribution be added as a second local head without losing the size advantage?
- How does Dettato perform on jazz, pop and non-Western corpora at the per-set level?
- Does the OMR parallel hold quantitatively, or is it an analogy from one practitioner's experience?
