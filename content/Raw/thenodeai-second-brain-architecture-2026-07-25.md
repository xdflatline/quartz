**Seven lessons** in summary:

1. **References always beat descriptions**, both in the technology and in the look.
2. **Small steps with approval, never everything at once.** That's what built the foundation and saved the look.
3. **Decide before you build.** The specification is the contract. Whoever builds first and decides later builds twice.
4. **Deterministic where possible.** Use AI only where real understanding is actually needed. That's why my indexer costs nothing and runs in seconds.
5. **Markdown remains the only truth.** No database, no format prison. Everything else is just a view onto your files and is replaceable at any time.
6. **Every claim needs a checkpoint.** Without the benchmark, to this day I wouldn't know whether any of this is even worth it. I would just believe it.
7. **The human decides, the AI delivers.** With every brainstorming question, with every contradiction in the wiki, with every visual step in this entire project, the AI did not make a single decision. It only made them possible.
