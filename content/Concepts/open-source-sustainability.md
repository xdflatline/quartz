## Overview

Open source sustainability refers to the factors that determine whether open source projects survive, thrive, or die. Despite their collaborative ethos, many OSS projects fail due to preventable organizational and social issues rather than technical ones.Source: hn-dumb-ways-for-an-open-source-project-to-die-2026-05-20

## Common Failure Modes

Based on community analysis (May 2026), common ways open source projects die include:

1. **Bus factor of 1:** Single maintainer with no succession plan
2. **Burnout:** Maintainer exhaustion from unpaid labor and community demands
3. **Governance disputes:** Conflicts over project direction, contributor rights
4. **Corporate capture:** Company takes over and alienates community contributors
5. **Dependency abandonment:** Critical upstream dependency dies, cascading failure
6. **License changes:** Switching from permissive to restrictive licenses drives away users
7. **Community toxicity:** Hostile maintainers or contributors drive away collaborators
8. **Funding gaps:** No sustainable revenue model for maintenance costs

## Patterns

- Projects often die slowly rather than abruptly — gradual decline in commits, issue response time
- The "dumb" in "dumb ways" refers to preventable, non-technical failures
- Community health is often more important than code quality for long-term survival

## Mitigation Strategies

- Multiple maintainers with clear governance structure
- Sustainable funding models (Open Collective, GitHub Sponsors, corporate backing)
- Clear contributing guidelines and code of conduct
- Regular contributor onboarding and mentorship
