# Three-Stage RL Curriculum for Temporal Reasoning

**Source:** Liu, Han, Yu, Li, You, *Time-R1* (UIUC, arXiv:2505.13508v2, 2025-06-03) ([[Raw/arxiv-time-r1-temporal-reasoning-2025-06-03]]), Section 3.2
**Category:** Architecture Pattern (training curriculum)
**Status:** Production-validated (peer-reviewed methodology with released checkpoints)

---

## Overview

The Time-R1 framework trains a moderate-sized LLM (3B parameters) for **comprehensive temporal reasoning** through a three-stage reinforcement-learning curriculum on a dynamic rule-based reward system. The three stages — Comprehension, Prediction, Generation — correspond to three increasingly forward-looking temporal capabilities, each building on the previous.

The key methodological claim is **progressive curriculum with transfer**: the third stage (creative scenario generation) requires no additional fine-tuning; the capabilities developed in stages 1 and 2 transfer via inference alone. Ablation shows that direct Stage 2 training without Stage 1 underperforms the full curriculum by 4.5 points (0.7331 → 0.7780).

## Core Content

### The three stages

| Stage | Goal | Input data | Training | Output |
|-------|------|------------|----------|--------|
| **1 — Comprehension** | Establish foundational temporal understanding via logical event-time mappings | Pre-cutoff historical (NYT 2016–2023, >200K articles) | RL on 4 subtasks | $\theta_1$ |
| **2 — Prediction** | Predict timing of future events after knowledge cutoff | Post-cutoff real + synthetic (Jan 2024 – Feb 2025) | RL on date prediction | $\theta_2$ |
| **3 — Generation** | Generate plausible, creative, time-anchored future scenarios | (none — inference only) | None; uses $\theta_2$ | Generated scenarios |

### Stage 1 — Comprehension: the four foundational subtasks

1. **Timestamp Inference** — infer `YYYY-MM` for an event from its headline + abstract
2. **Time-Difference Estimation** — infer dates of two events and the gap in months
3. **Event Ordering** — infer dates of three events and produce the correct chronological permutation
4. **Masked Time Entity Completion** — fill in a masked `<Year>` or `<Month>` token within event description

The model is forced to infer each event's date first, then produce the task-specific answer; both are scored. This prevents implicit guessing. Result: $\theta_1$ with robust foundational temporal understanding.

### Stage 2 — Prediction: future event time prediction

Initialized from $\theta_1$, the model continues GRPO fine-tuning on post-cutoff news (real Jan–Jul 2024 + synthetic Aug 2024 – Feb 2025 generated by DeepSeek-V3 from May–Jul 2024 news). Strict evaluation: fixed decay $\alpha = 0.1$. Result: $\theta_2$ specialized in future event time prediction.

The synthetic-data strategy is a deliberate anti-leakage measure: the test set is real news from the same period (Aug 2024 – Feb 2025), so training data for that period must be synthetic to avoid leakage.

### Stage 3 — Generation: creative future scenario generation (inference only)

$\theta_2$ is used for inference only. Three sequential steps:

1. **Generate** hypothesized news events for future months conditioned on 8 themes (Foreign Affairs, Business, Technology, Politics, etc.), each prompt requesting 3 unique items
2. **Filter** for diversity via greedy selection over `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` 384-d embeddings, yielding 5 high-diversity items per theme per month (40 total per month)
3. **Evaluate** plausibility via **AvgMaxSim** — mean over generated items of max cosine similarity to real news items in the target month

Stage 3 success **without any generation-task training** demonstrates that the S1+S2 curriculum builds transferable temporal reasoning.

### Why the curriculum works

- **Stage 1's foundational mapping** (event ↔ time) is the substrate. Without it, Stage 2 attempts to extrapolate from weak event-time understanding and produces worse predictions.
- **Stage 2's post-cutoff exposure** is what enables generalization. Pure within-cutoff training would not teach extrapolation.
- **Stage 3's inference-only transfer** is the payoff. The model has internalized enough about time and event structure to compose novel scenarios without explicit generative training.

The ablation data validates this hierarchy: Time-R1-S2-Direct (Stage 2 only) reaches 0.7331 on Stage 2 and 47.93% on Stage 3; full Time-R1 (S1+S2) reaches 0.7780 and 48.90%. Stage 1 contributes +0.045 (Stage 2) and +0.97 pts (Stage 3).

## Key Insights

1. **Progressive curriculum enables transfer.** The same model that learned to map events to times (Stage 1) and extrapolate (Stage 2) can generate (Stage 3) without explicit generation training.
2. **Foundational mapping is non-negotiable.** Stage 2 alone (without Stage 1) loses 4.5 points on prediction and 1 point on generation.
3. **Synthetic data can substitute for real future data** when the evaluation period is post-cutoff, provided the synthetic is generated from sufficiently recent context.
4. **Inference-only transfer is achievable** when the prior stages have built the right representations.
5. **Small + specialized > large + generalist** on temporally-grounded tasks. A 3B model with this curriculum beats 671B generalist models.
