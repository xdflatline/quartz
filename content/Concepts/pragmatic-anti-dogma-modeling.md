# Pragmatic Anti-Dogma Modeling (MMA Belief 9)

**Source:** Joe Reis, *The Mixed Model Arts Manifesto* ([[Raw/reis-mixed-model-arts-manifesto-2026-08-19]]), Belief 9
**Category:** Architecture Pattern (epistemic / methodological)
**Status:** Proposed best practice

---

## Overview

Reis's ninth and final MMA belief closes the manifesto by closing the loop on its central metaphor. The pragmatic instruction is: **understand the various data modeling approaches, why they're used, and where they're applicable — then pick what works**. Anti-dogma is not anti-rigor; it is the rejection of methodological tribalism in favor of first-principles selection by context.

The position is the philosophical twin of Kent's "data models are tools, not theories" (1978) and is the conclusion that the rest of MMA builds toward. If modeling is one discipline (Belief 1) and the fundamentals are unified (Beliefs 3–5), then the right tool for a given job is selected from the full set, not defended by tribal allegiance.

## Core Content

### Reis's specific instruction

> "Understand the various data modeling approaches, why they're used and where they're applicable. Pick what works for your situation."

This is deceptively simple. It commits the practitioner to:

1. **Knowing the full landscape.** Not just one school's canon.
2. **Knowing why each approach was created.** Every methodology has a context in which it solved a real problem.
3. **Knowing where each applies.** Every methodology has known failure modes.
4. **Selecting by context.** Choosing a methodology because it fits the situation — not because of inertia, identity, or recruitment availability.

### What pragmatic anti-dogma is not

- **Not "anything goes."** Selection is principled, not arbitrary.
- **Not "no standards."** Standards (e.g., third normal form as a baseline) are useful starting points.
- **Not "no opinion."** Experienced modelers have strong opinions about which tool fits which problem.
- **Not "no commitment."** A selected approach is committed to and followed through, not endlessly re-evaluated.

### What pragmatic anti-dogma is

- **First-principles selection.** Choosing a methodology because it fits the business reality and intent, not because of fashion.
- **Cross-school literacy.** Kimball practitioners who understand Data Vault; Inmon practitioners who understand dimensional; Data Vault practitioners who understand anchor modeling.
- **Methodology as toolkit.** Each methodology is a tool with known strengths and known failure modes — selected for the job, evaluated against the alternatives, retired when it stops fitting.
- **Identity decoupling.** Practitioner identity is not bound to a single school; "I'm a Kimball modeler" is a tribal marker, not a professional qualification.

### Connection to the MMA framing

This belief is the operational form of Reis's mixed martial arts metaphor. In the 1990s, MMA settled "my style beats your style" arguments by unifying all styles around fundamentals and adapting to context. Pragmatic anti-dogma is the data-modeling analogue: stop fighting the methodology wars, learn all the styles, pick the right one for each fight.

## Key Insights

1. **Pragmatism is principled, not arbitrary.** It requires more knowledge, not less.
2. **Anti-dogma is not anti-rigor.** Tribal allegiance and rigor are independent.
3. **Methodology as toolkit.** Each approach is a tool with known strengths, known failures, and known applicability envelopes.
4. **Identity decoupling.** The strongest practitioners are school-agnostic.
5. **MMA is the unifying frame.** "Train everything, apply what works" is the closing line of the manifesto and the operational form of the whole framework.
