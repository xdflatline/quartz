# Time-R1: Towards Comprehensive Temporal Reasoning in LLMs

**Authors:** Zijia Liu, Peixuan Han, Haofei Yu, Haoru Li, Jiaxuan You (UIUC Siebel School of Computing and Data Science)
**arXiv:** [2505.13508v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13508v2) [cs.CL] · v1 2025-05-16 · v2 2025-06-03
**License:** CC BY 4.0
**Code/data/models:** [github.com/ulab-uiuc/Time-R1](https://github.com/ulab-uiuc/Time-R1) · [Hugging Face collection](https://huggingface.co/collections/ulab-ai/time-r1-682626aea47cb2b876285a16)

## Abstract

> Large Language Models (LLMs) demonstrate impressive capabilities but lack robust temporal intelligence, struggling to integrate reasoning about the past with predictions and plausible generations of the future. [...] To address these limitations, we introduce Time-R1, the first framework to endow a moderate-sized (3B-parameter) LLM with comprehensive temporal abilities: understanding, prediction, and creative generation. Our approach features a novel three-stage development path [...]. Strikingly, experiments demonstrate that Time-R1 outperforms models over 200 times larger, including the state-of-the-art 671B DeepSeek-R1, on highly challenging future event prediction and creative scenario generation benchmarks. [...] we also release Time-Bench, a large-scale multi-task temporal reasoning dataset derived from 10 years of news data, and our series of Time-R1 checkpoints.

## Method overview

Time-R1 is built on **Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct** and trained with **GRPO** under a **dynamic, rule-based reward** across three stages:

1. **Stage 1 — Comprehension** on four pre-cutoff NYT-derived subtasks (timestamp inference, time-difference estimation, event ordering, masked time entity completion) → $\theta_1$
2. **Stage 2 — Prediction** of post-cutoff event dates (real Jan–Jul 2024 + synthetic Aug 2024 – Feb 2025 generated by DeepSeek-V3) → $\theta_2$
3. **Stage 3 — Generation** of plausible future scenarios across 8 themes × multiple months (**inference only**, no fine-tuning) → uses $\theta_2$

## Headline results

| Benchmark | Time-R1 (3B) | Best baseline | Margin |
|-----------|--------------|---------------|--------|
| Stage 1 Overall Avg. | **0.6476** | DeepSeek-R1-671B: 0.6916 | within striking distance |
| Stage 1 Completion | **0.7555** | DeepSeek-R1-671B: 0.7493 | +0.006 (best) |
| Stage 2 Future Event Prediction | **0.7780** | DeepSeek-R1-671B: 0.7503 | +0.028 |
| Stage 3 AvgMaxSim (Creative Generation) | **48.90%** | DeepSeek-V3-671B: 48.81% | +0.09 pts |

**Key claims validated by ablation:**

- Dynamic-reward curriculum produces higher accuracy AND ~2× more concise outputs (~130 vs ~250 tokens)
- Staged curriculum (S1+S2) outperforms Stage-2-direct training (S2 only): +0.045 on Stage 2, +0.97 pts on Stage 3
- Stage 3 success **without any generation-task fine-tuning** demonstrates transfer from Stages 1+2

## Core Contributions

1. **Unified temporal reasoning in one model:** first LLM with holistic temporal reasoning spanning logic, prediction, and generation
2. **Small model, big performance:** a 3B model matches/exceeds hundreds-of-billions-parameter models on temporal prediction and generation
3. **Fast adaptability and cost efficiency:** 3B can be quickly fine-tuned on new data; infeasible for hundreds-of-billions models
4. **Community resources:** Time-Bench (>200K examples) + Time-R1 model checkpoints

## Methodology highlights

- **Dynamic reward curriculum** (Section 3.3.3): stratify data by difficulty (initial checkpoint error ≤ 3 months = "easy"), then 3 phases: easy-only strict → full-task lenient → full-task progressively-strictened. The dynamic α transition prevents cold-start suboptimal convergence.
- **Synthetic future-event training** (Section 3.2.2): align baseline cutoff (DeepSeek-V3, July 2024), use real events from buffer (Jan–Jul 2024) + synthetic events generated from May–Jul 2024 context for evaluation period (Aug 2024 – Feb 2025). Avoids test-set leakage.
- **AvgMaxSim evaluation** (Section 3.2.3): for Stage 3 generative task, embed generated items + held-out real events with all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (384-d), compute mean over generated items of *max* cosine similarity to real events per month. Captures plausibility without requiring a single correct answer.

## Key Takeaways

- LLMs' temporal weakness is structural, not just data-bound: training corpora are static and processed non-chronologically.
- Specialized RL on small models can beat scale for temporally-grounded tasks.
- Progressive curriculum with transfer (S1 → S2 → S3 with S3 inference-only) is an underused pattern; the third-stage transfer is the most striking result.
- Rule-based verifiable rewards with dynamic curriculum are a clean alternative to learned reward models for tasks with continuous-distance ground truth.
