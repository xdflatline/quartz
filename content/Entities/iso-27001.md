# ISO 27001

**Category:** Compliance framework / international standard

**Issuer:** [International Organization for Standardization (ISO)](https://www.iso.org/) and [International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC)](https://www.iec.ch/)
**Standard reference:** [ISO/IEC 27001:2022](https://www.iso.org/standard/27001)
**Current version:** ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (supersedes 2013)

---

## Overview

ISO/IEC 27001 is the international standard for Information Security Management Systems (ISMS). It specifies the requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an ISMS within the context of the organization's overall business risks. Conformity means an organization has put in place a system to manage risks related to the security of data it owns or handles.

ISO 27001 is the most globally recognized information-security certification. It is widely required in enterprise procurement and is a common complement to (or substitute for) SOC 2 in non-US markets.

## The CIA Triad

The standard's foundation is the three principles of information security:

- **Confidentiality** — information is accessible only to those authorized.
- **Integrity** — information is accurate and unaltered.
- **Availability** — information is accessible when needed.

## Structure

ISO/IEC 27001:2022 has two main parts:

1. **Mandatory clauses (Clauses 4-10):** Define the ISMS requirements — context, leadership, planning, support, operation, performance evaluation, improvement.
2. **Annex A controls:** A catalog of 93 controls across four themes:
   - Organizational controls (37)
   - People controls (8)
   - Physical controls (14)
   - Technological controls (34)

The 2022 revision consolidated the previous 114 controls into 93 and added 11 new controls, including threat intelligence, information security for cloud services, and data masking.

## Certification

ISO 27001 certification is issued by an accredited certification body after a formal audit:

- **Stage 1 audit:** Documentation review.
- **Stage 2 audit:** On-site or remote audit of operating effectiveness.
- **Surveillance audits:** Annual.
- **Re-certification:** Every 3 years.

When a serverless GPU provider claims "ISO 27001 certified," the meaningful question is the scope of certification: does the certificate cover the specific product surface (serverless inference, fine-tuning, the data center where the workload runs)?

## ISO 27001 vs SOC 2

| | ISO 27001 | SOC 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Issuer | ISO / IEC | AICPA |
| Geography | Global | US-originated, increasingly global |
| Type | Certification (formal) | Attestation (auditor's report) |
| Framework | Prescriptive (clauses + Annex A controls) | Criteria-based (Trust Services Criteria) |
| Risk management | Required, central | Not required, but typically included |
| Output | Certificate | Report (Type I or Type II) |
| Procurement weight | Strong in EU, APAC, public sector | Strong in US enterprise |

Many cloud and SaaS providers hold both. They are complementary, not competing: ISO 27001 certifies the management system, SOC 2 attests the controls in operation.

## Why It Matters for Serverless GPU Procurement

- ISO 27001 is the global baseline. A provider without ISO 27001 will struggle to win enterprise or public-sector deals outside the US.
- The certificate scope matters. A provider's ISO 27001 certificate may cover the corporate ISMS but not the specific GPU cloud product; verify.
- The Statement of Applicability (SoA) lists which Annex A controls the provider has implemented. Customers with specific concerns (e.g., data masking, secure development) can ask for the SoA.
