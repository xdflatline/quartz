## Overview

*The Pragmatic Programmer: From Journeyman to Master* is Andrew Hunt and David Thomas's canonical 1999 book of software-engineering practice. Subtitled "From Journeyman to Master", it lays out a pragmatic philosophy of software development: care about your craft, take responsibility, fight entropy, invest in your knowledge portfolio, write DRY code, build orthogonal systems, use tracer bullets, prototype to learn, design by contract, test ruthlessly, automate everything, and sign your work.

The book is one of three that the operator has named as the canonical software-engineering reading list — alongside Evans's *Domain-Driven Design* and Ousterhout's *A Philosophy of Software Design*.

### Structure (8 chapters)

1. **A Pragmatic Philosophy** — care, responsibility, broken windows, knowledge portfolio.
2. **A Pragmatic Approach** — DRY, orthogonality, reversibility, tracer bullets, prototyping, domain languages, estimating.
3. **The Basic Tools** — plain text, shells, source control, debugging, text manipulation, code generators.
4. **A Pragmatic Paranoia** — design by contract, dead programs, assertive programming.
5. **Bend or Break** — decoupling, Law of Demeter, metaprogramming, temporal coupling, blackboards.
6. **While You Are Coding** — programming deliberately, algorithm speed, refactoring, testable code.
7. **Before the Project** — requirements pit, impossible puzzles, specification trap.
8. **Pragmatic Projects** — pragmatic teams, ubiquitous automation, ruthless testing, documentation, sign your work.

### The Quick Reference (70 tips)

The book's most-cited feature is its Quick Reference, a table of 70 numbered tips (Tip 1 through Tip 70). The tips are what practitioners cite by number during code review, in design discussions, and during grilling sessions. The strongest cross-cutting themes:

- DRY (Tip 11)
- Orthogonality (Tip 13)
- Broken windows (Tip 4)
- Tracer bullets (Tip 15)
- Prototyping (Tip 16)
- Domain languages (Tip 17)
- Design by contract (Tip 26)
- Ruthless testing (Tips 56–59)
- Sign your work (Tip 70)

The full table is on [[Concepts/pragmatic-programmer-tips]].

### The Drill Series

The book established a series of similar practical titles ("The Pragmatic Programmers" imprint), edited by Hunt and Thomas, that remains a strong imprint for software-engineering practical books. Notable titles in the series include *Pragmatic Unit Testing in C#*, *Pragmatic Unit Testing in Java*, *Ship It!*, and the *Pragmatic Starter Kit* series.

### Status

The book remains in print and is widely cited. It is one of the four titles the operator has explicitly named as the canonical software-engineering reading list. The 20th-anniversary edition (2019) is the current reference.
