## Overview

William Kent (Bill Kent) is an American database researcher best known for his short, philosophically inclined 1978 book *Data and Reality*, originally published by North Holland and republished by 1stBooks in 1998. He spent his career on the representation of information in computers — file organizations, indexes, hierarchical and network structures, relational and object models — and from that work concluded that all such structures are *maps* of an underlying *territory* that is amorphous, view-dependent, and ultimately resistant to formal capture.

He argued, controversially for the era, that data models are **tools** rather than **theories**: tools are incomplete, economic, useful, and orthogonal to the problems they solve; theories aim at completeness and distinction. Users of a model do not discover that their information "really is" hierarchical or relational — they learn to contort their problem to fit the tool.

## Key Details

- Author of *Data and Reality* (North Holland, 1978; 1stBooks, 1998)
- Influenced by S. I. Hayakawa (map/territory), Heinz Zemanek (philosophy of data), G. H. Mealy (theory of data), and Benjamin Lee Whorf (linguistic relativity)
- Active in database standards committees; contemporaries included Ted Codd, Chris Date
- Argued that durable conceptual models should be built from human-natural constructs, and that no two people's perceptions of reality are identical — reconciliation scales with **scope** (number of people) and **purpose** (breadth of the question)
- Sections on behavioral elements in *Data and Reality* are precursors of object orientation (per the Preface to the Second Edition, 1998)
- Brother George Kent, Political Science, University of Hawaii, contributed the book's perspective from outside computing

## Selected positions

- **The map is not the territory:** data structures are artificial formalisms; grammars don't describe the language we really use; formal logical systems don't describe the way we think.
- **No adequate formal modelling system exists:** information is too amorphous, ambiguous, subjective, slippery to be pinned down by deterministic processes.
- **Models as tools:** useful, incomplete, orthogonal to problems; not theories.
- **Viewpoint reconciliation:** depends on scope (people involved) and purpose (breadth); degrades as both grow.
- **Linguistic relativity of modelling:** nouns in our language shape which things we perceive as singular entities; the absence of a noun for a relationship renders the thought diffuse.
