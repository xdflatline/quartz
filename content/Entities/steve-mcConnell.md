## Overview

Steve McConnell is a software-engineering author and the founder of Construx Software. He is the originator of the "cone of uncertainty" model for software estimation error, which describes how the plausible error range of a software estimate narrows over a project's life as requirements and design decisions are actually made. The model is foundational to the argument for an upfront discovery phase: time alone does not narrow the cone, decisions do.

## Key Details

### The cone of uncertainty

McConnell's model states that at the initial-concept stage, a software estimate can plausibly be off by a factor of four in either direction. The range narrows only as concrete decisions (requirements, architecture, technical spikes) are made. Construx published the canonical white paper in 2019. The model underpins the article's recommendation that any pre-build estimate be presented as a range with stated assumptions, and that a discovery phase exists to make the narrowing decisions deliberately.

### Other works

McConnell is also the author of *Code Complete*, *Rapid Development*, and *Software Estimation: Demystifying the Black Art* — references cited widely in software-engineering practice.
