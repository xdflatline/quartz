# Eric Evans

**Source:** [[Raw/software-engineering-fundamentals-sources-2026-09-18]]
**Category:** People / Author
**Role:** Author of *Domain-Driven Design* (2003); founder of Domain Language, Inc.

---

## Overview

Eric Evans is an American software engineer and the author of *Domain-Driven Design: Tackling Complexity in the Heart of Software* (Addison-Wesley Professional, 2003), the canonical book that named and formalised Domain-Driven Design (DDD). The book introduced the three pillars of DDD — ubiquitous language, strategic design, and tactical design — and is one of three canonical software-engineering texts the operator has named on his reading list.

## Key Details

### Background

Eric Evans worked as a software engineer in financial trading and other complex domains through the 1990s. His experience with large, model-heavy systems led him to develop the DDD approach, which he articulated in a series of talks and writings before publishing the canonical book in 2003.

### Domain Language, Inc.

Evans founded Domain Language, Inc. (domainlanguage.com), a consultancy that provides DDD training, coaching, and assessments. The company runs public DDD workshops and private team engagements.

### The book

*Domain-Driven Design: Tackling Complexity in the Heart of Software* was published in 2003. The book is divided into four parts:

1. **Putting the Domain Model to Work** — the core thesis.
2. **The Building Blocks of a Model-Driven Design** — tactical patterns.
3. **Refactoring Toward Deeper Insight** — the practice of distilling the model.
4. **Strategic Design** — bounded contexts, context maps, large-scale structure.

The book is dense and difficult reading. Vaughn Vernon's *Implementing Domain-Driven Design* (2013) is the more accessible treatment that brought the ideas to a wider practitioner audience.

### The 2015 DDD Reference

In 2015, Evans published a free PDF reference summary of DDD's definitions and pattern summaries, available on domainlanguage.com. This document is often cited as the canonical short-form reference for DDD's vocabulary.

### Influence

Evans's influence on software engineering is broad:

- **DDD** is now industry-standard for large-scale system design.
- **Strategic design** (Part IV of the book) is the foundation of the microservices movement, of modular monolith design, and of most modern large-scale system design.
- **Ubiquitous language** has become a standard term for the practice of building a shared vocabulary between developers and domain experts.
- **Bounded context** has become a standard term for the explicit boundary within which a model is consistent.

### Reception

The book was difficult reading when first published, and the strategic-design chapter (Part IV) is the most-cited contribution. Critics argue that DDD requires a great deal of isolation and encapsulation to maintain the model as a useful construct, and Microsoft recommends DDD only for complex domains where the model provides clear benefits.

### Status

Evans remains active in the DDD community. He hosts DDD gatherings, runs workshops through Domain Language, and continues to write about DDD's evolution. He is one of the three canonical software-engineering authors in the operator's named reading list.
