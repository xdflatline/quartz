# Andrew Hunt and David Thomas

**Source:** [[Raw/software-engineering-fundamentals-sources-2026-09-18]]
**Category:** People / Authors
**Role:** Co-authors of *The Pragmatic Programmer*; Pragmatic Bookshelf co-founders; Agile Manifesto signatories

## Overview

Andrew Hunt and David Thomas are American software engineers and authors. They are best known as the co-authors of *The Pragmatic Programmer* (1999) and as the co-founders of the Pragmatic Bookshelf publishing imprint. The 70-tip Quick Reference at the back of *The Pragmatic Programmer* is the most-cited daily-practice reference for software engineering, and the book is one of three canonical software-engineering texts the operator has named on his reading list.

## Key Details

### Andrew Hunt

- Co-founded the Pragmatic Bookshelf with David Thomas in the early 2000s.
- Co-authored the Agile Manifesto (2001) with 15 other signatories.
- Co-authored *The Pragmatic Programmer* (1999) with David Thomas.
- Long-time advocate of pragmatic, craft-oriented software development.

### David Thomas

- Co-founded the Pragmatic Bookshelf with Andrew Hunt.
- Co-authored the Agile Manifesto (2001).
- Co-authored *The Pragmatic Programmer* (1999).
- Continued active role in the Pragmatic Bookshelf through the 2010s and 2020s.

### The Pragmatic Bookshelf

The Pragmatic Bookshelf (pragprog.com) is a publishing imprint co-founded by Hunt and Thomas. It publishes software-development and software-engineering books with a practitioner focus, including the *Pragmatic Unit Testing* series, *Ship It!*, *The Pragmatic Starter Kit*, and many others. The imprint has a distinctive visual style (the "pragmatic" tag) and a focus on practical, hands-on content.

### The Agile Manifesto

In February 2001, 17 practitioners gathered at Snowbird, Utah to draft the Agile Manifesto. Hunt and Thomas were among the signatories, joining Kent Beck, Mike Beedle, Arie van Bennekum, Alistair Cockburn, Ward Cunningham, Martin Fowler, James Grenning, Jim Highsmith, Andrew Koenig, Brian Marick, Robert C. Martin, Steve Mellor, Ken Schwaber, Jeff Sutherland, and Ron Jeffries.

### Influence

Hunt & Thomas's influence on practitioner software engineering is broad:

- The 70-tip Quick Reference is the most-cited practitioner reference.
- The book established a vocabulary (DRY, orthogonality, tracer bullets, broken windows) that is now industry-standard.
- The Pragmatic Bookshelf imprint has published dozens of influential software-engineering titles.
- The Agile Manifesto was signed by 17 people, of whom Hunt and Thomas were two.

### Reception of *The Pragmatic Programmer*

The book has been in print since 1999, with a 20th-anniversary edition published in 2019. It is widely cited in industry, recommended by practitioners, and translated into many languages. The 70 tips are referenced by number in code reviews and design discussions worldwide.
