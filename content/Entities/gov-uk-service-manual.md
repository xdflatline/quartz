# GOV.UK Service Manual

**Source:** [[Raw/devto-discovery-phase-software-development-2026-10-10.md]]

## Overview

The GOV.UK Service Manual is the UK government's reference for building digital public services, including its phases (discovery, alpha, beta, live). In the discovery-phase context, it is the canonical public-sector reference: it puts the principle plainly that discovery is about understanding the problem before committing to building anything, and that the team should not start building the service during the discovery phase.

## Key Details

### Discovery phase definition

Per the Service Manual, a discovery is the stage where the team understands the problem before committing to building anything, and where the team should not be building the service yet. The manual notes there is no set time period — around four to eight weeks is typical for public services, with a well-understood problem needing less. This is the same range the article uses for commercial software projects.

### Reframing guidance

A specific instruction the article lifts: when a project enters discovery with a predetermined solution, the team's job is to reframe it as a problem to be solved. The manual treats this as a routine step; the article applies the same guidance to most commercial projects.
